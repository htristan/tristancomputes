Yello! From first working prototype to beta.

Kids want their own phone. Parents want control over who can reach them. I led product direction for Yello! and built the first working prototype to make that experience tangible.

My role

Executive sponsor and product lead

Outcome

A new phone service for kids, launched into external beta by TextNow.

A phone kids can use. Boundaries parents can manage.

Yello! pairs a real phone number and network service with a parent portal. Parents approve contacts, and calls and texts are limited to those contacts in both directions.

The product problem spans two users and several systems: a child’s phone, the parent’s controls, and the network service enforcing those controls. Both experiences need to be clear.

Make the idea work

I built Yello!’s initial working prototype using AI-assisted development. It turned the product into an experience we could use and discuss.

As executive sponsor and product lead, I led Yello! through external beta. A cross-functional team delivered the production product.

Hold the beta until the product was ready

I made product quality and the protected phone environment release requirements. Any way for a child to get outside that environment had to be resolved before we put the product in families’ hands.

We held the beta release and pushed go-to-market back. I chose to give up the earlier launch date so we could ship a product we were proud to offer our customers.

A new product available to families

Yello! reached external beta with its own communication experience and parent portal. It is a concrete example of my work across product leadership, hands-on prototyping, and a consumer service that depends on telecom infrastructure.
